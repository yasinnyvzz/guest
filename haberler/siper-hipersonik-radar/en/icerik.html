ASELSAN is developing a Multi-Function Fire Control Radar to detect and track hypersonic targets for air defense systems that will sit above SİPER. According to information drawn from ASELSAN's magazine "Geleceğin Harekât Alanı ve Teknolojileri" (The Future Battlefield and Technologies), the work centers on X- and L-band radar fusion.

Why does the radar need "fusion"?

Hypersonic targets are hard to catch for two reasons. They can have a low radar cross-section, and the plasma that forms around them at high speed can make detection harder. According to the magazine, ASELSAN is working on X- and L-band radar fusion for these targets. Tracking the same target in different bands and combining the data is meant to fill in the track where a single band stays weak. No range, test date or delivery schedule has been shared for how this approach performs in the field.

The layer above SİPER

Reports say the radar is being developed for systems positioned in a higher tier above the SİPER air defense system. For SİPER's current status, the latest firings matter: last month SİPER Ürün-2 changed targets in flight and destroyed its new target, and SİPER Ürün-1 hit its target in a Turkish Air Force training shot at Sinop. The hypersonic radar work is described as a capability added on top of these two products, not a replacement for them.

What is happening on the ROKETSAN side?

According to the same source, ROKETSAN is working on the transition from solid-propellant propulsion to scramjet engines in its hypersonic projects. The aim is for the munition to gain high maneuverability in the terminal phase. Variable-flow fuel control system tests under the GÖKHAN project are also said to provide a foundation for more advanced engine technologies.

Not disclosed

  • The radar's range, operating frequency ranges and which platform it will be attached to
  • The prototype's first test date and entry-into-inventory schedule
  • Which missile product the radar will work with (which SİPER block, or a new product)

Why does it matter?

In air defense, the radar matters as much as the missile. Seeing a hypersonic target late can make even the best interceptor useless. That is why the real story here is the tracking chain, not the missile.

Source: ASELSAN, "Geleceğin Harekât Alanı ve Teknolojileri" magazine; Sanayi Gazetesi, 10 September 2026.
