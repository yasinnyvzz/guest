Kenya's military students are paid while they train, but the amount is not written down in any public Ministry of Defence document. The figures that circulate online for Kenya Defence Forces (KDF) trainees and cadets come from third-party salary sites, and they disagree with one another.

What is reported for KDF trainees and cadets

Salary aggregators repeat the same three numbers, usually without citing a source:

  • Ksh 7,172 a month for a recruit or cadet in training.
  • Ksh 11,852 a month for a cadet, in a separate listing.
  • Ksh 24,520 a month for an Officer Cadet after three years of service.

One of the pages carrying these numbers, Nairobi Law Monthly, presents them as fact. None of them points to a Ministry of Defence circular or a Salaries and Remuneration Commission (SRC) schedule, so they should be read as unconfirmed.

Why the figures cannot be checked

The Ministry of Defence recruitment notices set out age, KCSE grades, height and training length. They do not state a stipend. Envanter Media's own KDF salary guide makes the same point: there is no official public KDF pay scale. Allowances during training are described only vaguely in secondary sources, for example a risk allowance of about Ksh 5,000, again without a document behind it.

Who counts as a "military student"

The route matters, because it decides how long a trainee stays on training terms:

  • GSO Cadet (Regular): KCSE entry, three years of training toward a BSc in Defence and Security Studies.
  • GSO Cadet (Graduate): KCSE plus an eligible bachelor's degree, 15 months of training.
  • Specialist Officer: a six-month track for qualified professionals with at least two years' experience.

Details for each route are in the KDF cadet recruitment guide.

What applicants should do

Treat any exact stipend quoted by a third-party site as indicative only. Ask for terms in writing through the official channels, and remember that KDF recruitment is free. Anyone asking for money in exchange for a place, or offering a "calling letter", is running a scam.

The bottom line

Cadets and recruits receive pay during training, but no verified public figure exists for the allowance. Until the Ministry of Defence or the SRC publishes one, the numbers above remain reported estimates, not official rates.

Sources: Ministry of Defence (mod.go.ke) recruitment notices; Nairobi Law Monthly; Envanter Media archive.
