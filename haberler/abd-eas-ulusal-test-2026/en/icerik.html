The Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) and the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) are conducting a nationwide test of the Emergency Alert System (EAS) on Tuesday, November 17, 2026. The test will interrupt television, radio, cable and satellite programming for about one minute; phones will not receive an alert.

When and how the test runs

The test is scheduled for 2:20 p.m. Eastern (22:20 in Türkiye). Broadcasters interrupt regular programming to play a message of roughly one minute. According to officials, it reads: "This is a nationwide test of the Emergency Alert System, issued by the Federal Emergency Management Agency. This is only a test. No action is required by the public." Some broadcasters may carry it in both English and Spanish, and alert tones accompany the message for viewers and listeners who are deaf or hard of hearing.

What differs from 2023: no phone alerts

The last nationwide test, in October 2023, also included Wireless Emergency Alerts, so phones sounded too. This time only radio and television are involved. In FEMA's words, "your cell phone will not beep, buzz or receive a notification."

Why it is being held

Federal law requires a test of this kind at least once every three years. It lets officials find technical or operational problems before the system is needed in a real emergency. The EAS is a national public warning system made up of radio, TV, cable, satellite and wireline video providers, and through it the president can address the public during a national emergency. The same tones are used by local, state and tribal authorities for more localized alerts.

FEMA Administrator Cameron Hamilton said that "during an emergency, every second is crucial," and that the test will help identify and fix issues before a real event.

Answering the rumors

The timing sparked speculation online after several large outages hit banking, airline and cloud services in recent days. FEMA said the test is routine and unrelated to those disruptions, adding that there is no secret emergency. It had also noted that the test could be postponed if a real emergency or significant weather event occurred.
