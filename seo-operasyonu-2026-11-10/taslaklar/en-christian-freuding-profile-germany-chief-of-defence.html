Christian Freuding: Career, Ukraine Role and Plans of Germany's Next Chief of Defence

Lieutenant General Christian Freuding, 55, has commanded the German Army (Heer) since October 2025 and will become Generalinspekteur der Bundeswehr, Germany's Chief of Defence and highest-ranking soldier, in February 2027. Defence Minister Boris Pistorius announced the choice on 5 November 2026. Freuding succeeds General Carsten Breuer, whom NATO's chiefs of defence elected in September to chair the alliance's Military Committee. Before taking over the Army, Freuding led the defence ministry's Special Staff Ukraine, the hub for Germany's military aid to Kyiv.

Christian Freuding at a glance

  • Current post: Chief of the German Army (Inspekteur des Heeres), Army Headquarters in Strausberg, since October 2025.
  • Next post: Chief of Defence from February 2027; no exact day has been published.
  • Born: 20 August 1971 (official CV); Euronews gives Weiden in der Oberpfalz, Bavaria, as his birthplace.
  • Background: Armoured reconnaissance officer, political science graduate of the Bundeswehr University Hamburg, holder of a doctorate.

From reconnaissance officer to the minister's office

Freuding's early career ran through the armoured reconnaissance battalion in Lüneburg, where he rose from platoon leader to battalion commander, with an SFOR company command in Sarajevo and German and British staff courses in between. In 2007–2008 he was chief of staff of the ISAF Provincial Reconstruction Team in Kunduz, Afghanistan.

From 2009 most of his assignments were in the defence ministry in Berlin: policy planning on European defence, the office of the Chief of Defence and, from 2014 to 2019, the minister's own office, where he served as adjutant from 2017 to 2019, according to the ministry. His only brigade command followed, the 9th Armoured Brigade in Munster from 2019 to 2022.

Special Staff Ukraine and the planning staff

The defence ministry set up its Special Staff Ukraine directly after 24 February 2022 as the central hub for the government's military aid to Kyiv: matching Ukrainian requests with German deliveries, coordinating ministries and industry, organising training and drawing lessons from the war for the Bundeswehr. Freuding directed it from 2022 to 2023, according to his CV.

From 2023 to 2025 he directed the newly created Joint Planning and Command Staff (Planungs- und Führungsstab), while interviews in that period still presented him as the ministry's lead on Ukraine support. Reuters summed up the period as overseeing Berlin's arms supplies to Kyiv.

He also became one of the ministry's most visible officers; Euronews notes that some of his YouTube videos on the war passed one million views. In a November 2023 interview with Stuttgarter Nachrichten, reported by the Kyiv Independent, he said that "the fight for freedom has no expiration date" and that "every war ends at a negotiating table."

Pistorius cited this period when he named him Chief of Defence, saying that as head of the Ukraine special staff Freuding "analysed the rapid technical developments in Putin's war against Ukraine and drew conclusions for the Bundeswehr's ability to defend itself" (our translation).

Priorities as Army chief

Freuding took command of the roughly 63,000-strong Army from Lieutenant General Alfons Mais in Strausberg at the start of October 2025. In his first order of the day, he said he wanted to work for an Army "that is ready to fight, that prevails, that wins" (our translation).

The trade magazine ES&T listed the priorities he set out on taking command:

  • full equipment and personnel growth, active and reserve;
  • new and larger formations, including Panzerbrigade 45 in Lithuania;
  • rebuilding Army air defence and fighting with and against drones;
  • faster decision-making, with innovation as a leadership task.

Within a year, several had become concrete:

  • Lithuania brigade: On 4 February 2026 in Kaunas, Freuding placed the Multinational Battlegroup Lithuania, about 1,700 soldiers, under Panzerbrigade 45 as its third combat formation. The Bundeswehr expects the brigade to exceed 5,000 soldiers and reach full operational readiness before 2028.
  • Ukrainian instructors: In a March 2026 interview with Reuters, Freuding said Ukrainian soldiers would teach at German Army schools, describing Ukraine's forces as "the only one in the world with frontline experience against Russia."
  • Drone regiment: Pistorius announced on 1 October 2026 that the Army will start forming its first drone regiment in 2027, with 300–350 personnel at first and up to 600 later, under the 10th Panzer Division. Defense News reported a 2029 target for full operational capability.

Freuding ties these steps to a deadline. Speaking to Politico in June 2026, he said NATO allies agree Russia might be able to attack an alliance member in 2029: "2029 is not a German timeline. It's NATO-agreed intelligence." In an FAZ interview cited by ES&T, he said the Army was ready for a "fight tonight" despite personnel and materiel shortfalls.

What the Chief of Defence does in Germany

The Generalinspekteur is the Bundeswehr's highest-ranking soldier. Under the ministry's own description, the post holder:

  • is the disciplinary superior of all soldiers, and the armed forces are subordinated to him "in every respect";
  • serves as military adviser to the federal government and is part of the defence ministry's leadership;
  • is responsible for the overall concept of military defence, including planning and force development;
  • is responsible for leading the armed forces and for planning, preparing, commanding and reviewing Bundeswehr operations.

The current top-level structure is set by the Osnabrück Decree, in force since 1 May 2024, which separated the strategic, operational and tactical levels and created a single military command at the operational level.

What changes in February 2027

The handover completes a sequence that began in Copenhagen on 19 September 2026, when NATO's chiefs of defence elected Breuer, Chief of Defence since 17 March 2023, to succeed Italian Admiral Giuseppe Cavo Dragone as chair of the Military Committee. Breuer is due to take up the NATO post in summer 2027, a few months after Freuding's February start.

  • A new Army chief: The Army will need a new Inspekteur; the ministry's announcement did not name one.
  • A Ukraine-shaped agenda: Pistorius said Freuding would bring his skills to "the strategic direction of the Bundeswehr and its build-up with new capabilities" (our translation).
  • Army projects move up a level: The drone regiment, the Lithuania brigade and the 2029 readiness target will be overseen from the ministry rather than from Strausberg.

For the appointment itself, see our news report: Germany names Freuding next Chief of Defence as Breuer moves to NATO. Freuding inherits a force that has also had to raise its sabotage threat level to tier two, and that depends on large industrial commitments such as the €8.5 billion Rheinmetall 155mm ammunition contract for the Bundeswehr and Ukraine. Against drones, the Army is adding sensors and jammers such as 60 Thales GO12 portable radars and the Knifefish jammer on the Piranha 5.

FAQ

Who is Christian Freuding?

A German lieutenant general, born in 1971, who has led the German Army since October 2025 and directed the defence ministry's Special Staff Ukraine from 2022 to 2023.

When does Freuding become Germany's Chief of Defence?

In February 2027, according to the defence ministry's announcement of 5 November 2026. No exact date has been published.

Why is Carsten Breuer leaving?

Breuer was elected chair of the NATO Military Committee on 19 September 2026 and is due to start there in summer 2027.

What was Freuding's role on Ukraine?

From 2022 to 2023 he directed the Special Staff Ukraine, which coordinates Germany's military aid to Kyiv, and he remained a prominent voice on the war's lessons while heading the ministry's planning staff until 2025.

What has Freuding prioritised as Army chief?

Personnel and materiel growth, Panzerbrigade 45 in Lithuania, Army air defence, drones and counter-drone capabilities, and readiness by 2029.

Who will replace Freuding as Chief of the German Army?

The ministry had not named a successor as of 10 November 2026.
