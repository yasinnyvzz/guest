Taurus KEPD 350 Explained: Range, Warhead, Operators and Taurus Neo

The Taurus KEPD 350 is a German-Swedish air-launched cruise missile built by TAURUS Systems GmbH, a joint venture of MBDA Deutschland and Saab Dynamics. It weighs about 1,400 kg, carries a 480 kg two-stage MEPHISTO warhead designed to break into bunkers, and its makers give its range as "more than 500 km". Germany, Spain and South Korea have operated it for years. Sweden is adding it to the Gripen, and in December 2025 Germany ordered production preparation for an upgraded Taurus Neo.

Who builds Taurus and what the name means

TAURUS Systems GmbH (TSG), based in Schrobenhausen, Bavaria, was founded in 1998 by MBDA Deutschland and Saab Dynamics and is the weapon's prime contractor and design authority; a Seoul subsidiary followed in 2014. The partners do not currently publish the split, but Saab's 2002 announcement of the German series order gave Saab 33% and EADS-LFK (later part of MBDA Deutschland) 67%.

The Bundeswehr expands TAURUS as "Target Adaptive Unitary and Dispenser Robotic Ubiquity System" and KEPD as "Kinetic Energy Penetrator and Destroyer". The German journal ES&T says "350" is an internal project number, not a range figure.

How the missile works

Navigation that does not depend on GPS

MBDA's "Tri-Tec" package uses an inertial system updated by terrain-referenced navigation, image-based navigation (IBN) and GPS; TSG's factsheet adds an imaging infrared seeker and radar altimeter and markets the missile as GPS-independent. After release, Taurus drops, levels off and flies to the target at very low altitude, following the terrain.

MEPHISTO warhead and PIMPF fuze

MEPHISTO is a dual-stage warhead: a precharge clears the way for a kinetic penetrator built for hardened and deeply buried targets, with blast-and-fragmentation effect against area targets. The PIMPF programmable multi-purpose fuze counts layers and senses voids, so the penetrator can detonate on a floor chosen before launch.

Engine and speed

MBDA describes a high-thrust turbofan; German outlet hartpunkt identifies it as the US-built Williams P8300-15 and reports that a German alternative is being developed because of US export rules. Saab gives a speed of Mach 0.6–0.95.

Launch platforms

  • Tornado IDS (Germany): according to ES&T, this is still the only Bundeswehr aircraft cleared to fire Taurus as of December 2025.
  • Eurofighter: carriage trials flown, but electronic integration not done as of 2019 (TSG). The BAAINBw says Taurus Neo will be integrated on the Eurofighter; Spain plans Taurus for its new Halcón Eurofighters, El Español reported, citing Infodefensa.
  • EF-18 Hornet (Spain): operational. TSG said in 2018 that Taurus had been in the Spanish inventory for ten years.
  • F-15K (South Korea): on 12 September 2017 an F-15K-launched Taurus flew about 400 km to hit a target in the Yellow Sea (TSG).
  • Gripen C/D (Sweden): first live launch from a Gripen C at Vidsel on 21 August 2026; TSG announced successful integration in September 2026. Operational use is tied to the MS20 Block 4 upgrade expected in 2028 (Defence Industry Europe); Gripen E later.

Sweden is the newest user: its Armed Forces' 2024 annual report said FMV had opened talks with Germany, with initial capability on the Gripen C planned for 2028. In November 2026 Sweden took delivery of Taurus missiles from German stocks, in an undisclosed quantity, giving the air force a deep-strike capability it had lacked (see Sweden's military strength).

Taurus Neo: the next generation

In December 2024 Germany's procurement office BAAINBw awarded TSG a maintenance and modernisation contract to keep existing missiles in service into the 2040s; Saab's share was a SEK 1.7 billion order for 2025–2035. On 18 December 2025 the BAAINBw signed with TSG to prepare a series production line for Taurus Neo, to be procured through OCCAR so partner nations can join.

No value was published. Hartpunkt reported that the Bundestag budget committee had approved about €450 million the day before, around €415 million for production preparation; a plan for 600 Neo missiles at roughly €2.1 billion, reported by Der Spiegel in October 2024, is not contracted. ES&T says series production is planned from 2029. The goals – more range, better jamming resistance, improved seeker and navigation – are development targets, not proven capabilities. Spain will take part, defence minister Boris Pistorius said in August 2025, and Sweden plans Neo for the Gripen E. MBDA plans to invest €2.4 billion in 2025–2029 to expand production. A 2026 TSG flyer also promotes a "TAURUS Mk4" with "700+ km" range, without saying how it relates to the Neo order.

The Ukraine debate in Germany

Kyiv has repeatedly asked Berlin for Taurus, while Britain and France have supplied Storm Shadow/SCALP-EG since 2023. On 22 February 2024 the Bundestag rejected an opposition CDU/CSU motion that named Taurus, while approving a broader resolution on long-range weapons, AP reported. Chancellor Olaf Scholz said German soldiers "must not be linked" to the system's targets, arguing that targeting would need German personnel – a claim coalition critics disputed, The Irish Times reported. In March 2024 Russian state media published a leaked call in which Luftwaffe chief Ingo Gerhartz and other officers discussed Taurus.

Friedrich Merz backed deliveries in opposition. As chancellor he told Tagesschau on 1 July 2025 that Taurus "is and remains an option", with training taking at least six months. On 25 March 2026 he told the Bundestag that Ukraine now has its own long-range systems that are "significantly more effective than the relatively small number of Taurus cruise missiles we could provide", n-tv reported. On 4 October 2026 President Volodymyr Zelensky said he had raised Taurus with Merz again; Berlin has announced no supply decision. Britain, by contrast, is sharing classified Storm Shadow data and an assembly line with Ukraine.

On published figures JASSM-ER reaches furthest. Taurus and Storm Shadow are close in size and both pair tandem warheads with infrared seekers; Taurus stands out for its floor-selecting fuze and GPS-independent navigation. FAQ

What is the range of the Taurus KEPD 350?

More than 500 km, according to TAURUS Systems, MBDA and Saab; the Bundeswehr says "up to 500 km". No exact figure is published.

Who makes the Taurus missile?

TAURUS Systems GmbH of Schrobenhausen, Germany, a 1998 joint venture of MBDA Deutschland and Saab Dynamics.

Which countries use the Taurus missile?

Germany (Tornado), Spain (EF-18) and South Korea (F-15K); Sweden is adding it to the Gripen C/D.

What is Taurus Neo?

The upgraded successor. Germany signed for a production line on 18 December 2025; series production is planned from 2029, with the Eurofighter as carrier.

Taurus vs Storm Shadow: what is the difference?

They are similar in size. Taurus has the higher published range (over 500 km vs MBDA's "in excess of 250 km") and a layer-counting bunker fuze; Storm Shadow is combat-proven, including in Ukraine.

Has Germany sent Taurus to Ukraine?

No. In March 2026 Chancellor Merz said Ukraine no longer needs it. Kyiv continues to ask.
