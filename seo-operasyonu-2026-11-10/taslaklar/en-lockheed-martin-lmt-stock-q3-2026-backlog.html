Lockheed Martin Stock (LMT): Q3 2026 Results, a $292 Billion Backlog and the Post-Midterm Sell-Off

Lockheed Martin (NYSE: LMT) shares fell 6.9% on 4 November 2026, Reuters reported, after Democrats won control of both chambers of Congress and investors began to doubt the administration's $1.5 trillion defence request for fiscal 2027. The drop came less than two weeks after third-quarter results showed sales up 11% to $20.7 billion, earnings of $7.97 per diluted share, a record $292.0 billion backlog and a second consecutive increase in full-year guidance.

This article reports filings, market coverage and analyst statements. It is not investment advice. Share prices are dated.

What moved LMT stock in early November 2026

The trigger was the 3 November midterm result, not company news. In its 4 November report, Reuters said Lockheed fell 6.9%, L3Harris also lost 6.9%, Huntington Ingalls dropped 4.9% and RTX 3.1%. CNBC, reporting during the session, put Lockheed's decline at 3.6%; the closing loss was larger. According to 24/7 Wall St, LMT closed at $595.04 on 4 November, down $43.96 and its worst session since July 2025, yet still about 16.8% above its 30 September close of $509.25. Infodefensa reported on 6 November that Lockheed had lost close to 10% over two sessions, as Envanter noted in its piece on Rheinmetall's 16% fall in Frankfurt.

The worry is the size of the FY2027 budget. Reuters noted that the $1.5 trillion request compares with a currently approved level of about $901 billion, and that Jefferies analysts expect the final figure to land closer to the House's roughly $1.15 trillion. TD Cowen's Roman Schweizer said "Trump 'branded' programs are dead". Wolfe Research's Myles Walton told CNBC the sweep is "a headline negative" but potentially short-lived, with the FY2027 budget as the "near-term catalyst".

Net earnings rose 14% to $1.845 billion, and diluted EPS rose to $7.97 from $6.95. Free cash flow slipped to $3.0 billion from $3.3 billion on higher prior-year customer receipts. The 2026 outlook now calls for sales of about $80.0–82.0 billion (previously $79.75–81.75 billion), EPS of about $30.35–30.75 (previously $29.95–30.65) and free cash flow of about $7.8–8.0 billion (previously $7.0–7.2 billion). Lockheed bought back no stock in the first nine months of 2026, against $2.25 billion a year earlier; dividends paid rose to $2.41 billion.

Backlog and missiles: where the growth sits

Total backlog rose from $193.6 billion at the end of 2025 to $292.0 billion, and almost all of the increase is in Missiles and Fire Control, whose backlog tripled to $142.5 billion. The release ties $52.5 billion of the total to the multi-year PAC-3 MSE award. Lockheed's 29 July announcement valued that seven-year contract at up to $58.62 billion, aimed at tripling capacity by the end of 2030, and outlined $8–9 billion of investment through 2030 in more than 20 US facilities. Envanter has covered the seven-year PAC-3 MSE and THAAD production deals, the missile production investment plan and Boeing's $14.7 billion seeker contract.

Integrated air and missile defence sales, covering PAC-3 and THAAD, rose $480 million year on year in the quarter, and tactical and strike missiles, including GMLRS and PrSM, added $330 million. The Q3 release does not break out JASSM or LRASM, although the Navy is planning a sole-source deal to put LRASM on the B-52.

The F-35 funding risk in the filing

F-35 sales rose $710 million in the quarter, but deliveries fell to 43 jets from 46, and to 94 from 143 over nine months. The filing also carries a warning: Lockheed's costs on the Lot 20 advance acquisition contract "began to exceed the current contractual authorization and funding" in the third quarter, and without more US government authorization and funding, results, cash flows and financial condition "will be negatively impacted in future periods". Lot 20 and later contracts are still under negotiation. On results day the Pentagon and Lockheed also announced a 10-year framework for at least 850 F-35s, which still depends on Congress approving it and appropriating the money.

What the midterms change for the FY2027 budget

According to the Congressional Research Service, the continuing resolution in P.L. 119-103 funds the government through 11 December 2026. It bars new starts, production rates above FY2026 levels and new multiyear buys using economic-order-quantity advance procurement unless Congress funds them. CSIS breaks the $1.5 trillion request into roughly $1.15 trillion of discretionary funding and $350 billion through a reconciliation bill. The House Appropriations Committee's defence bill was about $1.072 trillion. Reconciliation passes on simple majorities, so that $350 billion depends on control of the new Congress, which sits from January 2027.

What to watch next

  • 11 December 2026: the current continuing resolution expires, according to CRS.
  • January 2027: the Democratic-controlled Congress takes office.
  • Q4 and full-year 2026 results: Lockheed has not announced a date. Last year it reported on 29 January, according to the company.
  • F-35 Lot 20: whether funding catches up with costs.
