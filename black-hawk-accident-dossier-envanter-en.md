# Black Hawk Accident Record: What Do the Numbers Say?

Sikorsky S-70 family helicopters in Türkiye have been involved in at least 23 fatal accidents since 1993. By this compilation, roughly 120-132 people died in them; the most recent was the S-70 crash in Malatya on 12 November 2026 that killed 4 soldiers. With more than 5,000 built worldwide, the Black Hawk is one of the most heavily used military helicopters. The absolute number of accidents is therefore high, while US Army data show a per-flight-hour accident rate lower than the Apache and the Chinook. A significant share of the causes cited in Türkiye relate to the flight environment: high-voltage lines, fog and bad weather.

## Summary

- **Türkiye:** Sikorsky helicopters operated by the Gendarmerie, Land Forces, Naval Forces and Police were involved in at least 23 fatal accidents with 132 deaths in this compilation. If one disputed record is counted as a single event, the figure is 22 accidents and 122 deaths. haber.aero's count, based on Flight Safety records, is 30 accidents including non-fatal ones and 118 deaths. The three deadliest: Siirt Pervari 2012 (17 killed), Hakkari 1998 (15-17 killed) and Tunceli Pülümür 2017 (12 killed).
- **Worldwide:** The Aviation Safety Network's H-60 family database lists 416 serious accident or write-off records and 1,003 deaths. The deadliest single event was the 67-fatality accident of 29 January 2025 in Washington, when a US Army UH-60L collided with a passenger aircraft.
- **Causes:** According to the US Army safety bulletin Flightfax, human factors were the primary cause in 83 percent of the 160 UH-60 accidents in 2016-2020, and mechanical failure in 17 percent. Among causes cited in Türkiye, wire or high-voltage line strikes (5), technical failure (5) and fog or bad weather (4) stand out.

## The Malatya Accident: Official Statement

At around 18:45 on Thursday 12 November 2026, an S-70 helicopter of the 2nd Army Aviation Regiment crashed in the Samanköy area of Yeşilyurt district, Malatya, near the Container City.

The Ministry of National Defence (MSB) announced that all 4 personnel aboard were killed: Pilot Major Erdem Çimen, Pilot Major Orhun Pınar, Technician Warrant Officer Senior Staff Sergeant Hüseyin Zehir and Technician Warrant Officer Senior Sergeant Serkan Önder. Two people on the ground were slightly injured by debris from the helicopter.

The ministry's statement: "Our S-70 helicopter crashed in Malatya for a reason yet to be determined, and our 4 heroic comrades aboard were martyred." The Malatya Chief Public Prosecutor's Office has opened an investigation.

## Causes of the Accidents

### Causes stated in Türkiye

| Cause category | Accidents | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Wire / high-voltage line strike | 5 | 22% |
| Technical failure | 5 | 22% |
| Fog / bad weather / low visibility | 4 | 17% |
| Accident during landing / low-level flight | 2 | 9% |
| Enemy fire | 1 | 4% |
| Not disclosed or under investigation | 6 | 26% |

A significant share of the stated causes relate to the flight environment: the mountainous terrain of the east and southeast, power lines, sudden fog and night operations. Military accident investigation reports are generally not shared with the public.

### Causes worldwide

- **Human factors dominate:** According to the January 2021 issue of the US Army safety bulletin Flightfax, there were 160 UH-60 accidents in 2016-2020; human factors were the primary cause in 83 percent and mechanical failure in 17 percent.
- **Night flights:** The 1996 Australia (18 dead), 2001 Hawaii (6), 2021 Philippines (6) and 2023 Kentucky (9) accidents all occurred on night flights. An Army report stated that the MH-60M that crashed in Washington state in 2025 struck three high-voltage lines during night training.
- **Weather and terrain:** Taiwan's Ministry of National Defense attributed the 2020 accident to weather, terrain and human factors; mechanical failure was ruled out.
- **Engine power loss:** Japan's final report on the 2023 Miyako accident described power loss in two engines in sequence; the exact root cause could not be determined.
- **Fuel:** Mexico's Attorney General's Office stated that the Los Mochis crash involved no outside attack or explosion and that the cause was fuel exhaustion.
- **Airspace arrangements:** The NTSB determined the probable cause of the Potomac collision to be the helicopter route's proximity to the airport approach path and the failure to mitigate that risk.
- **Conflict environment:** In the 2025 Amalfi accident in Colombia, a police Black Hawk was brought down in an attack. The method of the attack is disputed between sources.

## Accident Rates

- **Fleet size:** More than 5,000 Black Hawks have been built since 1979, and the fleet has accumulated over 15 million flight hours. Black Hawks make up 63 percent of the US Army's helicopter fleet.
- **Per-hour rate:** According to Army data reviewed by Military.com in April 2023, the Black Hawk had 1 serious accident per 100,000 flight hours in 2012-2022; in the same period the rate was 1.93 for the Apache and 1.59 for the Chinook.
- **Last five years:** According to a Defense One analysis of 31 January 2025, the Black Hawk had 12 serious accidents and a rate of 1.23 per 100,000 hours in 2020-2024; the Apache had 17 accidents and a rate of 4.41.
- **Design:** The UH-60A was the first operational helicopter designed to the US Army's crashworthiness criteria from the outset.

The data do not show the Black Hawk to be the riskiest helicopter in its class. Most accidents relate to mission conditions such as night flying, low altitude, mountainous terrain and bad weather.

## Türkiye's Sikorsky Fleet and the T-70 Programme

- **History:** The Turkish Armed Forces have operated the S-70 since 1989. Helicopters were acquired in groups of 10 in 1992-1994 and groups of 50 in 1999-2003. Operators are the Land Forces, Gendarmerie, Naval Forces (S-70B Seahawk), Police and the General Directorate of Forestry.
- **T-70 programme:** Under the Turkish Utility Helicopter Programme contracted in 2014, 109 T-70s derived from the S-70i were to be built under licence, with TUSAŞ as prime contractor and Sikorsky, ASELSAN, TEI and Alp Aviation as subcontractors. Planned distribution: Gendarmerie 30, Land Forces 22, Police 20, Forestry 20, Special Forces 11, Air Force 6.
- **Deliveries:** The first serial delivery was made to the Gendarmerie General Command on 13 December 2022. With this delivery the number of domestically produced helicopters TUSAŞ has delivered to the Turkish Armed Forces reached 100.
- **Production status:** Lockheed Martin's 10-K report filed with the SEC on 28 January 2025 stated that, because of the restrictions the US imposed on Turkish entities in 2020, work on the programme was partially halted as of 5 October 2024. Of the 109 helicopters, 38 had been produced.
- **Restart:** TUSAŞ General Manager Mehmet Demiroğlu said at the Farnborough show that export licences had been obtained for a new batch of 39 helicopters and that they aimed to restart production within 12-18 months.
- **Accident record:** No fatal T-70 accident was identified in this compilation. The fatal accidents involve the S-70A, S-70B and, in 2024, the S-70i.
