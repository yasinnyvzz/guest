# Iran's Nuclear and Missile Capabilities: News Package for Defence MENA (17 November 2026)

Editorial note: Items marked [VERIFY] rely on press reports or single sources and should be checked against primary sources before publication. Background framing draws on CFR's explainer "What Are Iran's Nuclear and Missile Capabilities?" (6 February 2026); the text below is original.

---

## Story 1
**Headline:** IAEA Cannot Locate Iran's 400+ kg of Highly Enriched Uranium

**Standfirst:** The agency says it cannot determine whether Iran's declared nuclear material has been diverted from peaceful use.

**Body:** According to an International Atomic Energy Agency report that surfaced in mid-November, the agency has lacked "continuity of knowledge" over Iran's nuclear material for more than a year. Its last stockpile estimate dates from June 2025: roughly 440.9 kg of uranium enriched to 60 percent and 184.1 kg enriched to 20 percent. These figures predate the strikes and are not a current measurement. Assessments of where the material sits diverge. Statements attributed to IAEA Director General Rafael Grossi suggest it is likely intact at Isfahan, while Iran's foreign minister said in March that it lies "under the rubble" of the 2025 attacks. [VERIFY: report text relayed through press coverage]

**Sources:** IAEA Board of Governors documents, The National, PBS NewsHour, Congressional Research Service

## Story 2
**Headline:** How Many Ballistic Missiles Does Iran Have? Estimates Range from 1,500 to 3,000

**Standfirst:** Figures vary sharply by source and date.

**Body:** U.S. intelligence assesses Iran's ballistic missile inventory as the largest in the Middle East. A 2022 U.S. Central Command estimate put it at more than 3,000 missiles. A 15 November assessment by an Israeli institute, citing U.S. intelligence, places the remaining inventory between 1,500 and 2,500. The gap may reflect strikes carried out since June 2025. CFR's missile backgrounder cautions that Western analysts cannot say with certainty how far Iran's technology has advanced, and that experts often doubt Tehran's claims on range and payload. [VERIFY: the 1,500–2,500 figure has not been confirmed against an official source]

## Story 3
**Headline:** Revolutionary Guards Say 2,000 km Missile Range Limit Lifted: What It Means

**Standfirst:** The announcement carries political signalling. It does not show that a missile with that range exists.

**Body:** Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps reportedly lifted its self-imposed 2,000 km range limit on ballistic missiles on 7 November. Analysts note that removing a cap does not mean missiles able to fly that far are ready. CFR reports that Iran has not fielded a missile capable of reaching the United States, though it continues to develop longer-range systems through its space-launch programme. [VERIFY: primary source for the 7 November statement]

---

## Story 4 (Explainer)
**Headline:** Why "Nuclear Missile" Is a Misleading Term: Iran's Two Separate Programmes

**Body:**
- **Nuclear programme:** enrichment levels, uranium stockpile, inspection access. The IAEA has reportedly had no verification access to enrichment sites since 28 February 2026. [VERIFY]
- **Ballistic missile programme:** range, payload, inventory, production capacity.
- **Where they meet:** a missile can carry a nuclear warhead only if a miniaturised warhead exists. There is no proof that Iran has produced one. Tehran maintains that its programme is peaceful.

## Story 5
**Headline:** Iran Rejects Shipping Enriched Uranium Abroad

**Body:** Iran has reportedly rejected a U.S. proposal to move its 60 percent enriched stockpile to a third country, with officials saying "not even a particle" will leave the country. Tehran also says inspections cannot resume while the threat of further strikes persists. [VERIFY: official statements come from a single source]
