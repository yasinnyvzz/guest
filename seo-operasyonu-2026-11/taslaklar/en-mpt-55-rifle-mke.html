MPT-55 Rifle: MKE's 5.56 mm National Infantry Rifle Explained

The MPT-55 is a 5.56×45 mm NATO infantry rifle developed by Turkey's state-owned MKE (Makina ve Kimya Endüstrisi) as the smaller-calibre member of the MPT-76 national infantry rifle family. MKE's catalogue lists the standard model with a 368.5 mm (14.5-inch) barrel, a short-stroke gas piston with a rotating bolt, a weight of 3,300 g without magazine, a length of 884 mm (stock open) or 800 mm (closed), 800 ± 100 rounds per minute and a 400 m effective range. It was introduced at IDEF 2017 and, according to Anadolu Agency, entered series production that year.

What is the MPT-55?

MPT stands for Milli Piyade Tüfeği, "national infantry rifle". The first rifle of the family, the MPT-76, is chambered in 7.62×51 mm NATO. According to an Anadolu Agency report published by CNN Türk in August 2019, MKE developed the MPT-55 using the experience gained with the MPT-76, in response to the need for a 5.56 mm infantry rifle. The same report says the rifle is intended in particular for built-up areas and close-quarters use.

The rifle uses a 30-round polymer composite magazine and a Picatinny rail for optics. The magazine release, bolt catch and safety can be used from either side, according to the same report. When the rifle was shown at IDEF 2017, The Firearm Blog described it as a select-fire, piston-operated 5.56 mm version of the MPT-76, presented together with the shorter MPT-55K.

MKE's small-arms range also includes the MAM-15 anti-materiel sniper rifle; see our coverage of MKE's MAM-15 deliveries and its second ammunition plant in Jordan.

How the MPT-55 relates to the MPT-76

The two rifles share the same operating principle, a gas-operated short-stroke piston with a rotating bolt, and the same family naming. The model numbers match the calibres: "55" for 5.56 mm and "76" for 7.62 mm. The MPT-76 is the heavier rifle of the pair. It was produced by MKE and, under a 2017 contract reported by Hürriyet Daily News, also by Sarsılmaz, which was to build some 10,000 rifles for the land, naval and gendarmerie forces. Turkish industry has also developed accessories around the family, such as 3E's AVCI combat optic built for the MPT-76.

Who uses the MPT-55?

MKE does not publish a user list for individual models; its catalogue says only that its rifles are used by the Turkish Armed Forces and allied military units. The most detailed public account is the August 2019 Anadolu Agency report carried by CNN Türk. According to that report:

  • the MPT-55 entered service in 2018 with the Presidential Protection unit, the Turkish Armed Forces, the police (Emniyet), the National Intelligence Organisation (MİT) and other security units;
  • under a contract between the Presidency of Defence Industries (SSB) and MKE, more than 33,000 long-barrel and more than 8,000 short-barrel rifles had been delivered, bringing the total in security-force inventories to over 41,000.

Outside Turkey, the Ukrainian outlet Militarnyi reported in May 2025, based on photographs released during a visit by Turkish Defence Minister Yaşar Güler, that Georgian special operations forces had been armed with MPT-55 rifles. No quantity was given, and we have not found an official Georgian statement.

FAQ

What calibre is the MPT-55?

5.56×45 mm NATO. Its sister rifle, the MPT-76, is chambered in 7.62×51 mm NATO.

What is the difference between the MPT-55 and the MPT-76?

They share the same short-stroke piston design, but the MPT-55 is smaller and lighter (3,300 g against 4,200 g) and uses 30-round 5.56 mm magazines. The MPT-76 has a longer 16-inch barrel, 20-round magazines and a 600 m effective range.

Who makes the MPT-55?

MKE, Turkey's state-owned Mechanical and Chemical Industry company.

What is the MPT-55K?

The short version, with an 11-inch (279.4 mm) barrel, a weight of 3,000 g and a 350 m effective range, according to MKE.

When did the MPT-55 enter service?

It was unveiled at IDEF 2017. Anadolu Agency reported that series production began in 2017 and that the rifle entered service with Turkish security units in 2018.

Is the MPT-55 exported?

Georgian special forces were reported in 2025 to be using it. We have not found official export figures.
