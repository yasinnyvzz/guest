HK416 Explained: Heckler & Koch's Piston-Driven 5.56 mm Assault Rifle

The HK416 is a 5.56×45 mm NATO assault rifle made by the German manufacturer Heckler & Koch (H&K) in Oberndorf. It keeps the general layout and controls of the AR-15/M4 family but replaces that design's direct-impingement gas system with what H&K calls an "indirect gas-operated" mechanism: a short-stroke gas piston driving a locked rotating bolt. The current HK416 A5 is offered with 11-, 12.5-, 14.5-, 16.5- and 20-inch barrels, takes 10-, 20- or 30-round magazines and has a quoted rate of fire of 850 rounds per minute. Norway, France, Germany and the US Marine Corps (as the M27) are its best-documented military users.

What is the HK416?

H&K markets the HK416 as a modular rifle platform for armed forces and police. According to the manufacturer's product page, the rifle has a full-length NATO STANAG 4694 Picatinny rail for day and night sights, controls that can be operated from either side, a tool-free gas adjustment that allows a suppressor to be fitted at any time, and a magazine well optimised for M4/M16-pattern magazines. H&K also states that the rifle passes the NATO barrel-obstruction test and can be fired immediately after surfacing from water, a feature aimed at maritime special forces.

The company says the Norwegian Armed Forces were the first customer to adopt the HK416, in 2007. In January 2019 H&K announced a further Norwegian order for HK416 rifle sets worth about €22 million, spread over 36 months. The rifle regularly appears in comparisons of current service rifles, including Envanter Medya's ranking of the best service rifles of 2026.

Short-stroke gas piston vs AR-15 direct impingement

The main engineering difference between the HK416 and a standard AR-15, M16 or M4 is how propellant gas cycles the action.

  • Direct impingement (AR-15/M4): gas tapped from the barrel travels back through a thin tube straight into the bolt carrier, where it expands and pushes the carrier rearward. The system is light and has few parts, but hot gas and carbon fouling are deposited inside the receiver and on the bolt.
  • Short-stroke piston (HK416): gas tapped from the barrel pushes a short piston in the gas block. The piston and operating rod strike the bolt carrier and then stop, and the gas is vented at the front of the rifle instead of entering the receiver. H&K describes this as indirect gas operation.

The practical case for the piston is a cleaner, cooler bolt group and better tolerance of the extra back-pressure created by suppressors, which is why the HK416 has an adjustable gas regulator. The trade-off is additional mass towards the front of the weapon and a proprietary gas system rather than standard AR-15 parts. The same short-stroke principle is used by several newer rifles, including Turkey's MPT-55 from MKE and the SAR 56 from Sarsılmaz, according to their manufacturers' published data.

United States: the M27 Infantry Automatic Rifle

The US Marine Corps uses an HK416-based rifle as the M27 Infantry Automatic Rifle, carried by squad automatic riflemen. In August 2017 Military.com reported that the Corps intended to negotiate a sole-source deal with Heckler & Koch for up to 50,814 additional M27s, and described the rifle as weighing about eight pounds with a 30-round magazine. The report presented wider issue to infantry riflemen as an option under discussion at that time, not a final decision.

France: HK416F

France chose the HK416F under its AIF (Arme Individuelle Future) programme to replace the FAMAS. According to Shephard, the procurement agency DGA signed the contract on 22 September 2016 for a first firm tranche of 5,340 rifles worth about €168 million; Defense News reported that the first 400 rifles were received on 3 May 2017 and that more than 100,000 were planned over roughly ten years. The French Ministry of the Armed Forces describes the HK416F as coming in a standard version for ground troops and a short version for airborne troops, weighing about 4 kg, and deployed operationally since 2018.

Germany: G38, G95 and G95A1

Germany uses several HK416 designations. A 2014 VISIER report said the HK416 A5 had been catalogued as the G38, with the 11-inch model listed as the G38C. On 10 October 2017 the BAAINBw ordered 1,745 HK416 A7 rifles, designated G95, for the Kommando Spezialkräfte (KSK) and the navy's special forces. In January 2023 the BAAINBw announced the start of procurement of the HK416 A8-based G95A1 as the Bundeswehr's new standard rifle, with the G95KA1 short version for specialised forces, and said deliveries would begin in 2026. The selection had been made in spring 2021 but was delayed by a legal review.

Norway

Norway was the first national adopter in 2007, according to H&K, and placed a follow-on order in 2019. Other users are frequently reported in the press, but this page lists only those confirmed by manufacturer or government statements. FAQ

Is the HK416 an AR-15?

It shares the AR-15/M4 layout, controls and magazine type, but it uses a short-stroke gas piston instead of the AR-15's direct-impingement gas tube, so its gas system and upper receiver are H&K designs.

What calibre is the HK416?

5.56×45 mm NATO, with 10-, 20- and 30-round magazines according to H&K. H&K's 7.62×51 mm NATO counterpart is a separate model, the HK417.

What barrel lengths does the HK416 come in?

H&K lists the HK416 A5 with 11-, 12.5-, 14.5-, 16.5- and 20-inch barrels, plus German G38 models in 11 and 14.5 inches.

What is the G95?

The G95 is the Bundeswehr designation for the HK416 A7 bought for special forces in 2017. The G95A1, based on the HK416 A8, is Germany's new standard rifle, with deliveries announced from 2026.

What is the difference between the HK416 and the M27?

The M27 is the US Marine Corps' designation for its HK416-based infantry automatic rifle, configured for the squad automatic-rifleman role.

Which countries use the HK416?

Confirmed military users include Norway, France (HK416F), Germany (G38, G95, G95A1) and the US Marine Corps (M27). Other users reported in the press should be checked against official sources.
